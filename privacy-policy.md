# Solray Studio Privacy Policy

Effective 12 October 2026. This policy covers Solray Studio 1.0.0 for Windows.

## Who makes Solray Studio

Solray Studio is a free app made by Code du Soleil. In this policy, "we" and "us" mean Code du Soleil, and "Solray" means the app. You can reach us at codedusoleil@gmail.com.

## The short version

Solray works on your computer and nowhere else. It has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and it never sends what you play, record, open or make over the internet. Solray gives us no information about you.

## What Solray uses on your computer

- Your audio input: a microphone, an audio interface or another recording device you pick. Sessions, the Tuner, Challenges and the delay measurement in Settings listen to it while they are on screen, and Sessions records from it when you press Record. What Solray hears is analysed in memory and then let go; only your recordings are kept, and only on your computer.
- Your MIDI keyboard, when one is connected: the notes you play light up and sound in the Visualizer. They are not stored.
- The files you open: audio files you import, separate or transcribe, MIDI files, session folders and a separation model you import for Stems. Solray reads them where they are. It copies them only into a session you save, a song into the folder its stems are written to, and a separation model into its own folder (below).
- The names of your audio and MIDI devices, graphics cards and processor, to list them and to choose where Stems runs. They stay on your computer.

## What Solray keeps on your computer

- Sessions you save, with their recordings: in Documents\Solray Studio\Sessions, unless you save them somewhere else.
- Songs you export go where you choose, by default Music\Solray Studio. MIDI files you export go where you choose, by default Documents\Solray Studio\MIDIs.
- Stems go to the folder you pick, by default Music\Solray Studio, with a copy of the song they were separated from.
- Your last transcription in Tabs, with the location of the track it came from.
- The separation model you imported for Stems, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Solray Studio\Stems models. Remove it in the Stems section, or delete that folder.
- Your settings: the tuning, sound and volume, the audio devices you picked (by their names), the folders you used, your recent sessions, the audio delays you measured, how long separations took on your devices, and the options you chose last. These and your last transcription are kept in %APPDATA%\com.codedusoleil\Solray Studio.

None of this is sent anywhere. You can delete any of it at any time. Uninstalling Solray removes the app but leaves your sessions, exports, and stems where they are; delete those folders to remove them too.

## What can leave your computer

Solray itself sends nothing. Two things outside Solray can:

- Microsoft components. Solray runs its Stems and Tabs models with ONNX Runtime and DirectML, which are made by Microsoft. Like other parts of Windows, they can record usage events in Windows' own diagnostic data, which Windows sends to Microsoft as far as your choices in Settings > Privacy & security > Diagnostics & feedback allow. These events describe how the components run, not your music or your files. Microsoft's Privacy Statement covers them: https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement
- The Microsoft Store, if you got Solray from it, and Windows' own error reporting. Microsoft runs both, under the same Privacy Statement.

## Your controls

- Windows decides which apps may use your microphone: Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone.
- Solray opens your audio input while a section that listens is on screen (it starts on Sessions), and closes it soon after you move to one that does not. You pick the input on the toolbar of any section that listens.
- Everything Solray keeps is in the places listed above, and you can delete what you like.

## Children

Solray collects no personal information from anyone, children included.

## Changes

If Solray starts handling information differently, this policy will change first, with a new effective date at the top. The current version is always in the app, in Settings > Documents.
